Pick a direction

3 directions for the launch video of Orbit, inside the video style Midnight Glow. Each is a different idea, not a different colour; the render under each is its own signature moment. Change the video style.

Direction A
One Link, Lands
warm, immediate, sure

The whole video is told through the invite card itself: one button press, three status pills changing state, nothing else performs. The signature move is a literal morph of the product's own Copy link button into the filled roster, repeated at the hook, the proof, and the close so the viewer learns the shape of the product by watching one control three times.

Signature move: the cursor presses Copy link and the card's own Invited pill morphs in place into Joined, the roster filling before the viewer's eyes
Its signature moment, rendered: Watch It Fill

The moments

  1. The Chase: a line of text that sounds like something they'd mutter to themselves
  2. One Link: the product's name and its whole method in two words
  3. Watch It Fill: pressing the button is the whole action; the roster fills itself
  4. One Workspace: joining lands everyone in the same shared place, not a separate inbox
  5. No Chasing: the pain named in the hook is explicitly closed
  6. Start Free: the call to action in the product's own words
Details: how it was judged
Serves the claim
yes, the entire video is built from the product's own invite card performing the exact claim, nothing illustrative stands in for it
Brand at its best
yes, it trusts the product to be the whole show and uses the site's own warm accent as the hero colour against the style's dark ground
A competitor could use it
no, the specific card, its exact row labels and the Invited-to-Joined morph are Orbit's own UI, not a generic invite flow
Survives a bad day
yes, one camera move, one control, large type; it reads on a phone with sound off and survives a flat render because nothing depends on a complex composite

Closing shot
the invite card at rest, all three rows reading Joined, Start free glowing warm beneath it
Direction B
The Empty Seats
sparse, anticipatory, filling

Instead of showing the product UI early, the video opens on the abstract shape of the problem: three empty seat outlines, warm light pooling where people should be. The signature move is the seats themselves filling, one by one, carrying the literal shape of 'your whole team in' through the whole video rather than the invite card chrome.

Signature move: a single seat outline becomes Jamie's filled Owner seat, then Sam's filled Joined seat, then Morgan's, the same shape relaying through three real roles
Its signature moment, rendered: Seats Fill

The moments

  1. Three Empty Seats: three faint outlines, unlit, with the pain line arriving letter by letter above them
  2. One Link: the method named simply, right as the seats begin to matter
  3. Seats Fill: one press by Jamie, and two outlines fill in sequence with real names
  4. Full Workspace: all three seats filled and warm, framed as a complete set
  5. Not One At A Time: the pain point is directly answered
  6. Start Free: the action and the mark, in that order
Details: how it was judged
Serves the claim
yes, the seat metaphor is a direct visual restatement of 'your whole team in' that stays literal to the claim without naming a number it can't cite
Brand at its best
yes, it takes the site's plain, warm confidence and gives it a spatial shape that still ends on the real product UI
A competitor could use it
no, the three-seat relay is built from Orbit's own role labels (Owner, Joined, Invited) and its exact seat-limit framing, not a generic team metaphor
Survives a bad day
yes, the shapes are simple enough to render cheaply, read large on a phone, and the orbit camera is forgiving of a lower frame rate
Score
8 / 10
Opening hook
Three empty seats, and an inbox full of unanswered emails.
Closing shot
three filled seats glowing warm in a row, the Orbit mark small beneath them, Start free beside it
Direction C
Status Line
crisp, live, typed

The whole video treats the workspace as a running status log: lines of text typing in and updating in real time, each line a real product state (Invited, Joined, roles set). The signature move is a token-swap on one fixed sentence whose accent word cycles through the product's own feature names, making the video feel like the product's own activity feed talking.

Signature move: one fixed line, 'The workspace updates live:', whose accent word cycles through three real feature names as the log ticks
Its signature moment, rendered: Updates Live

The moments

  1. Another Email: a single quoted line, like a real message, typing itself out
  2. One Link: the product name appears as a small persistent mark beside the first log entry
  3. The Log Fills: two new lines type in, each naming a real teammate and their new status
  4. Updates Live: one fixed sentence whose last word cycles through three real feature names
  5. One Invite: the pain is directly closed in the product's own log style
  6. Start Free: the final log line is the call to action itself
Details: how it was judged
Serves the claim
yes, every line in the log is the claim restated in the product's own words and states, nothing decorative stands between the viewer and the words
Brand at its best
yes, it leans into 'live' and 'simple', two of the site's own tone words, more directly than the other two directions
A competitor could use it
no, the exact token-swap words (Shared documents, Seat limits, Works on mobile) and the exact log lines (Sam Kim — Joined) are Orbit's own feature and sample names, not generic
Survives a bad day
yes, it is the cheapest to render of the three (flat ground, locked camera, type only), and reads instantly on a phone with sound off since it's built from text a viewer can read
Score
8 / 10
Opening hook
Another email: 'did you get the invite?'
Closing shot
the status log's final line reading Start free, cursor blinking once, then still
